Guidance for choosing K

K (trough width parameter, i = K·z₀) reflects how "peaked" or "wide" the settlement trough is, and depends primarily on the ground type above the tunnel crown:

Ground conditionTypical K rangeNotes
Stiff / fissured clay (e.g. London Clay type)0.4 – 0.5Most widely quoted default for stiff clay is K ≈ 0.5
Soft to firm clay0.5 – 0.7Wider, shallower troughs than stiff clay in some cases
Sand / gravel, above water table0.2 – 0.3Narrower, more peaked trough
Sand, below water table / loose0.25 – 0.35Confirm with local case-history data where available
Weathered rock / residual / mixed ground0.3 – 0.5Engineering judgment; consider layering above crown

K is entered manually in this tool — the table above is reference guidance only. Where possible, calibrate K against local monitoring / case-history data for the specific ground and method rather than relying on generic ranges.

Assumptions & limitations
  • Transverse settlement trough only, computed for the long-term / steady-state condition after the tunnel face has advanced well beyond the section considered. Longitudinal (face-advance) settlement profile is not covered by this tool.
  • Greenfield conditions are assumed — no allowance is made for the stiffening or load-shedding effect of existing structures, piles, or buried services within the zone of influence.
  • Depth to tunnel axis (z₀) is derived from Natural Ground Level, Road/Rail level, and the distance from Road/Rail level to invert, as: z₀ = NGL − (RL − distance + D/2). Settlement is computed relative to Natural Ground Level, i.e. the actual ground surface, not the Road/Rail formation level — check this is the intended reference surface where NGL and RL differ (e.g. embankment or cutting).
  • Volume loss Vl is taken as constant along the drive and is a direct user input; it is not derived or predicted by this tool.
  • Ground is treated as reasonably homogeneous between tunnel axis and ground surface. Significant stratification or a shallow stiff/soft interface is not explicitly modelled — engineering judgment on K is needed in such cases.
  • Trough width parameter K is assumed constant with depth and applies to tunnels of moderate to typical cover (z₀ ≳ 1.5–2D). For very shallow tunnels the Gaussian assumption becomes less reliable.
  • For twin tunnels, the combined trough is obtained by simple linear superposition of two independent Gaussian troughs (New & O'Reilly, 1991). This is known to potentially under-estimate settlement over the pillar between closely spaced tunnels (centre-to-centre spacing ≲ 2D) due to soil-arching / stress-redistribution effects between bores; numerical modelling is recommended to verify closely-spaced twin-tunnel cases.
  • Horizontal ground displacement is estimated assuming displacement vectors point towards the tunnel axis (O'Reilly & New, 1982) — this is best supported for cohesive soils; granular soils may not follow this assumption exactly.
  • Output covers vertical settlement, slope, horizontal displacement, and settlement contour distances only. A full Burland/Boscardin-type building-damage classification and serviceability limit checks are outside the scope of this tool.
  • This tool gives a preliminary, greenfield estimate suitable for planning-stage screening. For any structure identified as being within the zone of significant ground movement, a project-specific soil–structure interaction assessment is required.
