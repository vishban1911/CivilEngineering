Method & assumptions (read before use)
This tool checks surface crack width for a rectangular section under combined axial force (P) and bending moment (M) at service (unfactored) loads, then applies IS 456:2000 Annex F.
  • Section response: a cracked, linear-elastic strain field is solved by iterative force/moment equilibrium (concrete carries no tension; steel and concrete are linear-elastic). This automatically captures the benefit of axial compression — larger P shifts the neutral axis and reduces (or removes) the tensile strain.
  • Uncracked case: if equilibrium shows the whole section in net compression, no flexural crack forms and the check reports a governing crack width of 0.
  • Multiple layers: up to 3 layers on the tension side and 3 on the compression side, each at its own depth, all participate in the equilibrium solve (except any layer marked "distribution"). The effective depth d used in Annex F is the area-weighted centroid of the tension-side layers. Layer 1 (tension side) — the layer nearest the tension face — governs the acr / crack-width geometry.
  • Distribution/secondary steel (perpendicular to the main bars, for shrinkage & temperature control) is excluded from the M–N equilibrium solve and from the acr calculation.
  • Effective depth is never entered directly — each layer's depth is derived from its own clear cover and bar diameter.
  • Tension stiffening: the strain at the tension-steel centroid is reduced to εm per Annex F eq. F-2 before use in the crack-width formula.
  • Long-term modulus: if sustained loading applies, Ec,eff = Ec / (1 + θ), θ = creep coefficient (IS 456 Cl 6.2.5.1 gives typical θ = 2.2 / 1.6 / 1.1 for loading ages of 7 days / 28 days / 1 year — θ is project- and age-dependent, so it's entered directly here).
  • Steel grade (fy) does not enter the Annex F crack-width formula — Annex F works directly with the elastic strain field, not with yield strength. Steel grade is used only for the separate SLS permissible-stress check.
  • Scope: single rectangular section, uniform cover per layer. Not valid for flanged, circular, or non-prismatic sections.
